# Related work — the literature this thesis sits inside

Compiled August 2026. This is a **reading list organised by argument**, not an
alphabetical bibliography: each entry says what it is cited *for*, and where it
differs from what MASID does. `RESEARCH-LOG.md` §"Related work" holds the
earlier, grey-literature-heavy pass; this document is the peer-reviewed spine
that a defence panel will expect underneath it.

**The one-line thesis of the whole literature:** the procurement side has a
mature quantitative standard, the imagery side has a mature technical
literature, and *nobody has joined them over the same contracts and reported
which side failed.* That join, plus the null result, is the contribution.

Entries marked ⚠️ have a citation detail I could not verify from a fetchable
source — confirm before putting them in a bibliography.

## 0. The near-neighbour — read this first, and differentiate hard

- **GIST — Ghost Infrastructure Spectral Tracker.** Yale environmental data

  <https://github.com/tdfrago/yale-environmental-data-science-capstone>

  Same 248k BetterGov dataset, same Sentinel-2 + Google Earth Engine stack, same

  **What FINDINGS §6 does to it.** Our measured null at 10 m says a

  sharpest thing this thesis has to say about prior work, and it is a testable
  claim rather than a complaint. Say it carefully and support it with

## 1. The methodological ancestor — measuring what is missing, not who is guilty

- **Olken, B. A. (2007). "Monitoring Corruption: Evidence from a Field
  Experiment in Indonesia." *Journal of Political Economy* 115(2), 200–249.**
  <https://www.journals.uchicago.edu/doi/10.1086/517935> ·
  [NBER w11753](https://www.nber.org/papers/w11753) ·
  [free PDF](https://www.povertyactionlab.org/sites/default/files/research-paper/27_Olken_Monitoring_Corruption.pdf)

  **The most important citation in this document.** Olken had engineers dig core
  samples out of 600+ Indonesian village roads, independently price the
  materials actually present, and difference that against the official
  expenditure report. The gap — "missing expenditures" — is a quantity, not an
  accusation. Audits raised from 4% to 100% of projects cut it by eight
  percentage points; grassroots monitoring alone did not.

  **Why it is the ancestor.** It establishes the entire move this thesis makes:
  *compare the physical record against the paper record and report the
  discrepancy as a measurement.* Our FINDINGS wording — "the record disagrees
  with itself" — is Olken's epistemics with a satellite substituted for the
  core sample.

  **And it is also the strongest statement of our ceiling.** Olken needed to
  physically excavate to reach quantity. FINDINGS §1 measured that 64.9% of
  contract value is invisible to any camera — the same barrier, from orbit.
  Cite Olken *for* the method and *against* over-claiming from imagery.

## 2. Procurement red flags — the standard your bidding tier will be measured against

- **Fazekas, M., Tóth, I. J., & King, L. P. (2016). "An Objective Corruption
  Risk Index Using Public Procurement Data." *European Journal on Criminal
  Policy and Research* 22(3), 369–397.** DOI
  [10.1007/s10610-016-9308-z](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10610-016-9308-z)

  The canonical CRI. Composite of contract-level red flags — single bidding
  foremost — validated *indirectly*, by showing high-CRI firms have higher
  profitability, higher contract-to-estimate ratios, more politically connected
  ownership and more tax-haven registration.

  **Read the validation strategy, not just the indicators.** This is the field's
  answer to exactly our problem: no itemised ground truth. They validate a risk
  score against *correlates* of corruption rather than against confirmed cases.
  That is a defensible template for validating our 2×2 triage.

- **Fazekas, M., & Kocsis, G. "Uncovering High-Level Corruption: Cross-National
  Objective Corruption Risk Indicators Using Public Procurement Data."
  *British Journal of Political Science*.**
  <https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/british-journal-of-political-science/article/abs/uncovering-highlevel-corruption-crossnational-objective-corruption-risk-indicators-using-public-procurement-data/8A1742693965AA92BE4D2BA53EADFDF0>
  ⚠️ *volume/year unverified*
- **Fazekas, M., & Tóth, I. J. (2016). "From Corruption to State Capture."
  *Political Research Quarterly*.**
  <https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/abs/10.1177/1065912916639137>
- **IMF (2022). "A Method to Assess Corruption Risks in Public Procurement."**
  <https://www.elibrary.imf.org/downloadpdf/view/journals/001/2022/094/article-A001-en.pdf>
- **Open Contracting Partnership — Cardinal**, open-source red-flag library over
  OCDS; and *Red Flags in Public Procurement* (73 indicators with formulas).
  <https://www.open-contracting.org/resources/red-flags-in-public-procurement-a-guide-to-using-data-to-detect-and-mitigate-risks/>

  **Anticipate this question at the defence:** *why six hand-rolled checks
  instead of an existing 73-indicator standard?* The honest answer is already in
  your data and it is a fact, not a preference — PhilGEPS publishes no bidder
  counts and no ABC column, so single-bidding and bid-to-ABC (the two indicators
  carrying most of the CRI's weight) **are not derivable at all**. Say that,
  cite Fazekas for what you would have computed, and move on.

### 3c. Change detection — the CS half of the thesis

- **Chen, H., & Shi, Z. (2020). LEVIR-CD** — 637 VHR (0.5 m) bitemporal pairs,

  Building Change Detection." *Remote Sensing* 13(24), 5094.**
  <https://www.mdpi.com/2072-4292/13/24/5094> ·

  **S2Looking matters more than LEVIR-CD for you.** It is deliberately rural,

  should not be expected to transfer here.

- **Broad-area search of new construction using Landsat/Sentinel-2 time series.**

## 4. SAR — the evidence base for Tier 3, the one lever still open

- **Gu, H., Tang, X., Cho, K., Acord, A. J., Rasmussen, P. G., Bosch, M., &
  Woodcock, C. E. (2026). "Detection of New Construction Activities with
  Sentinel-1 and Landsat Time Series."**
  <https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S2352938526000261> ·
  [SSRN](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=5253458)

  **This is the paper that justifies building the SAR tier.** Detects new
  building construction from Sentinel-1 backscatter time series at **F1 = 0.83
  (recall 0.87, precision 0.79)**, and improves to F1 = 0.84 fused with a
  Landsat-based algorithm. Same free 10 m sensor class that failed optically for
  us — different physics, an order of magnitude better result.

  Two caveats to state honestly when you cite it: it detects *buildings*, which
  are strong corner reflectors, and a revetment is not one; and it is a fusion
  of radar with optical time series, which is structurally the same argument
  this thesis makes about tiers.

- **Coherence change detection with Sentinel-1 for natural and anthropogenic
  disaster monitoring in urban areas.**
  <https://www.researchgate.net/publication/326040372_>
- **"SAR coherence change detection of urban areas affected by disasters using
  Sentinel-1 imagery." *ISPRS Archives* XLII-3, 1857 (2018).**
  [PDF](https://isprs-archives.copernicus.org/articles/XLII-3/1857/2018/isprs-archives-XLII-3-1857-2018.pdf)

  Method detail worth copying: they aggregate coherence to **street blocks** and
  take the standard deviation over the whole stack rather than differencing one
  pair. Our bootstrap-null-from-an-annulus design is the same instinct; theirs
  is citable prior art for it.

- **PS-InSAR monitoring of construction-induced ground deformation, Dangjin,
  Korea.** *Int. J. Applied Earth Observation* (2022).
  <https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0303243422000472>

## 5. Super-resolution — the literature that backs `superres_eval.py`

- **JRC (EC Joint Research Centre). *AI Super-Resolution of Satellite Imagery.***
  [PDF](https://publications.jrc.ec.europa.eu/repository/bitstream/JRC143067/JRC143067_01.pdf)
- **"Hallucination Score: Towards Mitigating Hallucinations in Generative Image
  Super-Resolution."** [arXiv:2507.14367](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.14367v2)
- **"Multi-image super-resolution in remote sensing: A review of AI techniques
  and challenges."** *ISPRS/Adv. Space Research* (2026).
  <https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0094576526002766>

  **These say, in the literature's own voice, what `superres_eval.py` was built
  to test.** Two quotable positions: scaling factors above **4×** are generally
  avoided in operational EO because of reliability concerns — S2DR3 is **10×**;
  and validation must be **task-aware**, because spectral-fidelity metrics are
  passed perfectly by a model that hallucinates and then downsamples
  consistently. That is precisely the critique in your script's header, and you
  can now attribute it rather than assert it.

## 6. Satellite data as governance evidence — the framing precedent

- **Hodler, R., & Raschky, P. A. (2014). "Regional Favoritism." *Quarterly
  Journal of Economics* 129(2), 995–1033.**
  <https://academic.oup.com/qje/article-abstract/129/2/995/1867438> ·
  [VoxEU summary](https://cepr.org/voxeu/columns/regional-favouritism-across-world)

  38,427 subnational regions, 126 countries, 1992–2009: a leader's birth region
  emits measurably more nighttime light. **Cite it for the epistemic move, not
  the method** — it is the standard reference for *satellite data as independent
  evidence about the allocation of public money*, precisely because pixels are
  not reported by the actor being audited. That is the one-sentence
  justification for this entire thesis, and it is already in a top-five
  economics journal.

- **World Bank, *Light Every Night* nighttime lights dataset.**
  <https://blogs.worldbank.org/en/opendata/light-every-night-new-nighttime-light-data-set-and-tools-development>

## 7. Philippine literature — local, and more useful than expected

### 7a. COA — and a correction to an assumption in this repo

**Standing assumption to revise:** the repo states in several places that no
itemised public ghost list exists, so the triage can only be an *ordering*.
**That is now only partly true.** COA has filed multiple Fraud Audit Reports on
**DPWH Bulacan 1st DEO specifically** — our exact case study — and press
coverage carries itemised amounts, named contractors and named officials.

- COA turns over 4 FARs, ₱351 M ghost projects, Bulacan — *Manila Bulletin*, Oct 2025
  <https://mb.com.ph/2025/10/02/coa-turns-over-to-ici-4-fraud-audit-reports-on-p351-m-ghost-flood-control-projects-in-bulacan>
- ₱279 M flagged ghost or relocated — *Philstar*, Nov 2025
  <https://www.philstar.com/headlines/2025/11/29/2490731/p279-million-bulacan-flood-control-projects-flagged-ghost-or-relocated-coa>
- ₱344 M ghost/mismatched — *GMA*, 2025
  <https://www.gmanetwork.com/news/topstories/nation/965797/coa-finds-p344-m-bulacan-flood-control-projects-as-ghost-mismatched/story/>
- 4 FARs over ₱275 M — *BusinessWorld*, Feb 2026
  <https://www.bworldonline.com/spotlight/2026/02/16/730771/coa-files-4-fraud-audit-reports-worth-over-p275-million-for-bulacan-flood-control-projects-flags-ghost-projects-unauthorized-relocations-and-questionable-accomplishments/>
- COA submits FARs to ICI — *Rappler*
  <https://www.rappler.com/philippines/luzon/coa-fraud-audit-report-bulacan-flood-control-projects-ici/>
- 747 unusable/idle DPWH projects, ₱6.5 B, 2024 — *Philstar*
  <https://www.philstar.com/headlines/2025/12/04/2491664/coa-finds-747-unusable-idle-dpwh-projects-2024>

**Three things in there that bear directly on the thesis:**

1. **Named contractors — SYMS Construction Trading and Wawao Builders** — and
   named officials. Both firms are joinable against our 176-contractor register.
   That is a partial validation set, obtained without an FOI request.
2. **COA's inspection teams used satellite imagery themselves.** The state
   auditor already treats imagery as admissible support for a fraud finding.
   That is a significance argument and a methodological precedent at once.
3. **The failure modes COA names are the ones our flags encode** — ghost
   structures, *unauthorised relocation*, and **payment for structures that
   existed years before the contract**. Relocation is our
   coordinate-contradicts-description flag. Pre-existing structure is exactly
   what a before/after Wayback pair tests, and it is a *better* framing of the
   imagery tier than "is it there" — because the pre-existing case is invisible
   to any single-date check.

**Action item:** try to obtain the FAR documents themselves rather than the
press summaries, via COA's website or the ICI. Even 8–10 contract-level labels
would convert the triage from an ordering into something with a measured
precision at the top of the ranking. Note the caveat honestly — a set drawn from
what COA chose to audit is not a random sample, so it bounds precision, not
recall.

## 9. Framing — what a ghost project *is*

- **Third World Quarterly special issue, "Ghost Projects — Ruined Futures and

  Aalders, eds.; 2026). Lead article:
  <https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/01436597.2025.2610335>

  Twelve contributions on what ghost projects *mean*, none on how to *detect*
  them. The literature has theory without method; this thesis has method with a

## The gap, stated as a table

| strand | has | lacks |
|---|---|---|
| Olken 2007 | physical ground truth, causal design | scale; needs excavation |
| Fazekas CRI | validated procurement indicators at scale | no physical delivery check |
| S2Looking / CD literature | strong methods at ≤0.5 m | no procurement link, no PH data, benchmark-only |
| Gu et al. 2026 (SAR) | free-sensor construction detection that works | buildings, not revetments; no audit framing |
| AidData GIE | geocoding rigour, evaluation methods | outcomes, not delivery verification |
| GIST | same data, same country | single sensor; no procurement tier; no null reported |
| TWQ special issue | theory of ghost projects | no detection method |
| COA FARs | confirmed cases, itemised | not a random sample; not machine-readable |
| **MASID** | **all four tiers over the same 1,293 contracts, with the failing tier reported** | **validation labels — the binding constraint** |

**Read first, in this order:** Olken 2007 → Fazekas et al. 2016 → Gu et al. 2026
→ the COA FAR coverage → S2Looking. Those five carry the argument.
